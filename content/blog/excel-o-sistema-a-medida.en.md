---
title: "Excel or a system? 7 signs you've outgrown your spreadsheet"
description: A spreadsheet is the best tool to start with, and it's often enough forever. How to tell when it stopped being enough, and why the next step isn't always a custom-built system.
---

Almost every business starts the same way: an Excel or Google Sheets spreadsheet for inventory, another for orders and another for what people owe you. And that's perfectly fine: it's free, anyone knows how to use it and you can change it in a minute.

The problem isn't the spreadsheet. It's that the business grows and the spreadsheet stays the same, just with more and more tabs, more colors and more "don't touch that column".

We say this even though we build custom systems: **many businesses don't need one**. But there are fairly clear signs of when it does make sense to take the step.

## The 7 signs

### 1. There are several versions of the same file

"Inventory_final.xlsx", "Inventory_final_v2.xlsx", "Inventory_final_THIS_ONE.xlsx". When two people work on different copies, neither one is the truth. And the day someone overwrites the other person's changes, nobody knows which number to believe.

### 2. The inventory in the spreadsheet doesn't match the stockroom

If every so often you have to count everything to "adjust" it, the spreadsheet is no longer a record: it's an estimate. And selling something you don't have (or not selling something you do have) costs money and customers.

### 3. You copy data from one place to another

From WhatsApp to the spreadsheet, from the spreadsheet to the invoice, from the invoice to the collections spreadsheet. Every copy is wasted time and a chance for error. If someone on your team spends hours a week copying and pasting, there's a cost there you're not seeing.

### 4. Only one person understands the formulas

The spreadsheet works because "Juan built it". If Juan goes on vacation or leaves the business, nobody knows why that cell gives the result it does. It's the same risk as [depending on a developer who disappears](/en/blog/programador-desaparecio-que-hacer), but inside your own business.

### 5. You can't set permissions

In a spreadsheet, whoever can see it can see all of it: costs, margins, salaries. And whoever can edit it can delete everything. If you need the salesperson to see prices but not costs, the spreadsheet won't let you.

### 6. You don't know who changed what

A price changed, a row disappeared, an order shows as delivered and it wasn't. Without a clear history of who made each change, every mistake ends in an argument.

### 7. Decisions come too late

You want to know which product sells the most, which customer has owed you for more than 60 days or how much you sold this month compared to the same month last year. And to find out, someone has to spend an afternoon putting together a summary. When the information comes late, you decide blind.

## If one or two apply to you, you don't need a system yet

Before paying for development, there are in-between steps that solve a lot for little money:

- **Moving to Google Sheets**, if you still use Excel on a single computer: everyone works on the same file, there's version history and you can share it as view-only.
- **Organizing the spreadsheet**: one tab per thing, validation so data can't be entered wrong, protected cells.
- **Forms** to enter orders or transactions, instead of typing straight into the spreadsheet.

## If three or more apply to you, look at an off-the-shelf system

For most common needs (inventory, invoicing, customers, appointments) there are **off-the-shelf management systems** you pay for monthly. They're quick to set up and someone else takes care of maintaining them. For many businesses, that's the right step.

What to check before choosing one:

- That you can **export everything** (your data has to be able to leave).
- That it has **per-user permissions**.
- That it connects with what you already use: your online store, electronic invoicing, Mercado Pago.
- How much it costs when you add users or your volume grows.

## When a custom system makes sense

Building your own makes sense when:

- **The way you work is what sets you apart** and no system accounts for it (per-customer pricing, your own production processes, a particular order workflow).
- You're paying for **two or three systems** that don't talk to each other and the spreadsheet is still the glue.
- What you pay per month in licenses, plus the hours of copying and pasting, already exceeds what it would cost to have something of your own.

If you go down that road, do it with a clear contract: [the 9 clauses](/en/blog/clausulas-contrato-programador) and [the code in your name](/en/blog/quien-es-dueno-del-codigo).

## One piece of advice for any path

Before switching tools, **write down how you work today**: what comes in, who enters it, what goes out and who uses it. It's one page. That page works just as well for organizing the spreadsheet, choosing an off-the-shelf system or asking for a custom quote. And without it, any new tool will inherit the mess of the old one.
