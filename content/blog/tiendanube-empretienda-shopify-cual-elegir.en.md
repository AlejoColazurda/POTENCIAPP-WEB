If you're going to open your online store in Argentina, you're almost certainly choosing between these three: **Tiendanube**, **Empretienda** and **Shopify**. All three work well. The difference is in what happens after you open: how much they charge you per sale, how you get paid, how you ship and what you do the day you need something the platform doesn't include.

We work with all three and we don't earn a commission from any of them, so this comparison doesn't lean either way.

**Before you read on:** plans and fees change often. We don't list prices here because they'd be out of date in a couple of months. Open each one's pricing page while you read, and to work out the full cost use [this cost guide](/en/blog/cuanto-cuesta-tienda-online).

## Tiendanube

It's the most widely used platform in Argentina and in much of Latin America. It was born here, so it's designed around how people buy and sell here.

**Pros:**

- Native integration with **Mercado Pago** and local payment methods, installments included.
- Shipping with local postal and courier services, plus its own shipping solution.
- Lots of apps and lots of people who know how to use it: it's easy to find help.
- Everything is paid in pesos.

**Keep in mind:**

- Depending on the plan, on top of the monthly fee it charges a **percentage on every sale**. On the more expensive plans the percentage goes down. With volume, that fee is what weighs most in the math.
- The apps you add often have their own monthly fee. Check how many you have every so often.

**Who it's for:** the business that wants to sell seriously, with a variety of products and shipping nationwide, and prefers a local platform with support in Spanish.

## Empretienda

It's also Argentine, and it's simpler. It became known for a fixed-fee model, aimed at entrepreneurs who are just starting out.

**Pros:**

- A very simple dashboard: if you've never had a store, you'll have it running in no time.
- It integrates with Mercado Pago and the most common local shipping options.
- Priced in pesos and easy to predict. Check on its website how it charges per sale today, because that's exactly what sets it apart.

**Keep in mind:**

- It has fewer integrations and fewer design options than the other two.
- If the business grows a lot or you need something specific (wholesale customers with different prices, a large catalog, a connection to your inventory system), you may outgrow it sooner.

**Who it's for:** the business that's just starting, with a small catalog, and wants a simple fixed cost.

## Shopify

It's the biggest platform in the world. It's powerful and has thousands of apps, but it isn't designed for Argentina.

**Pros:**

- Very flexible: design, automations, selling in several countries and currencies.
- The largest ecosystem of apps and developers there is.

**Keep in mind:**

- The monthly fee is charged **in dollars**, by card. In Argentina that adds the taxes and surcharges applied to purchases in foreign currency.
- Its own payment processor doesn't work in Argentina. When you get paid through an external method, like Mercado Pago, Shopify usually adds **its own transaction fee**, on top of the payment method's fee. Check on its website how much it is for the plan you choose.
- Most apps are also paid in dollars.

**Who it's for:** the brand that sells or will sell abroad, or that needs something local platforms can't handle and has a budget in dollars to sustain it.

## How to decide in five questions

1. **Where are your customers?** If they're in Argentina, Tiendanube or Empretienda. If you're going to sell in dollars or abroad, Shopify starts to make sense.
2. **How much will you sell per month?** Do the math with your own numbers: monthly fee + platform fee + payment method fee + apps. With low volume, the fixed fee wins. With high volume, the plan that charges less per sale wins.
3. **How many products and variants do you have?** A large catalog with lots of sizes and colors calls for a more complete platform.
4. **Do you need to connect it to something?** Your inventory system, your invoicing or Mercado Libre. Check which integrations exist **before** choosing, not after.
5. **Who's going to run it?** If it's just you, simplicity is worth a lot.

## What about a custom store?

For most businesses that are starting out, **a platform is the right decision**, and we say so even though we build custom stores. A store of your own starts to make sense when the per-sale fees already weigh more than the cost of maintaining it, or when you need something no platform does well.
