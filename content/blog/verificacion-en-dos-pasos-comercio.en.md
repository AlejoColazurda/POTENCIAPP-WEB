Almost every account theft we see in small businesses starts the same way: someone gets the password (because it was reused, because it leaked or because it was typed into a fake link) and gets in. **Two-step verification** stops that: even if they have the password, to get in they also need something only you have, usually a code on your phone.

It isn't foolproof, but it stops the vast majority of attacks. And it takes ten minutes to turn on.

## First, which method to choose

Almost every app will offer you some of these options. From most secure to least secure:

1. **Passkey**: you sign in with your fingerprint or your phone's screen lock. There's no code anyone can ask you for or steal. If the app offers it, use it.
2. **Authenticator app** (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator or similar): it generates a new code every 30 seconds, on your phone, without internet. It's the best option when there's no passkey.
3. **Prompt on your phone** ("Are you trying to sign in?"): convenient and fairly secure, as long as you **don't tap "Yes" if it wasn't you**.
4. **SMS**: it's better than nothing, but it's the weakest. If someone manages to get a duplicate of your SIM card from the phone company, they receive your codes.

## WhatsApp (and WhatsApp Business)

In WhatsApp it's called **two-step verification** and it's a six-digit PIN you choose. It's asked for every so often and, above all, when someone tries to register your number on another phone.

- Go to **Settings → Account → Two-step verification → Turn on**.
- Choose a PIN that isn't your date of birth or "123456".
- **Add an email address**: it's how you reset the PIN if you forget it.

This is what prevents the most common scam: "I sent you a code by mistake, can you pass it to me?". Even if someone gets that registration code, without your PIN they can't take over the account. We cover it in full in [WhatsApp and Mercado Pago scams](/en/blog/estafas-whatsapp-mercado-pago).

## Gmail and your Google account

Your Google account is the most important of all: with your email you can recover almost every other account.

- Go to **myaccount.google.com → Security**.
- Turn on **2-Step Verification** and, if your phone supports it, create a **passkey**.
- Add the **authenticator app** as a second method.
- Download the **backup codes** and keep them printed out, away from your computer.
- On the same page, check **your devices** and sign out of any you don't recognize.

## Instagram and Facebook

Both are managed from Meta's **Accounts Center**.

- On Instagram: **Settings → Accounts Center → Password and security → Two-factor authentication**.
- Choose the account and the method: **authentication app** over SMS.
- Save the **recovery codes** it offers you.
- If several people manage your social media, each one should log in with **their own user** through the business portfolio, not with the main account's password.

If it's already happened to you, [my business's Instagram got hacked](/en/blog/hackearon-instagram-negocio) has the step-by-step guide to get it back.

## Mercado Pago and Mercado Libre

This is the account where the money is, so it's the one that deserves the most care.

- From the app: **Your profile → Security** (the exact menu name changes a bit between versions) and turn on **two-step verification**.
- Check the **open sessions** and the **devices** where your account is signed in.
- Set a password to open the app and turn on fingerprint unlock.
- If an employee needs to see sales or take payments, use the **account permissions** or the team tools your plan includes. Don't give them your login.

## Also: the bank, the store and the domain

The same logic applies to everything that runs your business:

- **Online banking**: almost every bank has a token or an in-app prompt. Turn it on.
- **Your online store** (Tiendanube, Empretienda, Shopify): all of them allow two-step verification for the dashboard. We have a [security checklist for stores](/en/blog/proteger-tienda-tiendanube-empretienda).
- **The domain registrar and the hosting**: if someone gets in there, they can take down your website and your email.

## So you don't lock yourself out

The most common fear is "what if I lose my phone?". For that:

- **Keep the backup codes** for each account, printed out, in a safe place.
- Have **two methods** set up whenever you can (for example, authenticator app + passkey).
- If you change phones, **move the authenticator app first**, before wiping the old one. Most have an option to export or sync.
- Keep the **recovery email and phone number** for each account up to date.

## The rule with no exceptions

**Never give a code to anyone.** Not to "Mercado Pago support", not to "the bank", not to a customer, not to an acquaintance who says they sent it to you by mistake. Real companies never ask you for that code: if someone asks you for it, it's a scam.
