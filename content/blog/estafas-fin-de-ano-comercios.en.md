---
title: December, the month of scams: 5 that target businesses during the holidays
description: Fake accounts running giveaways, the customer who "overpaid you", the supplier who changed their CBU. How to recognize them and what to have set up before December 1.
---

In December you sell more, you serve customers faster and there are more new people on the team. Scammers know it: it's the month when a business is most rushed and checks the least. None of these scams is new, but during the holidays they show up a lot more.

These are the five most worth keeping on your radar, and what to do about each one.

## 1. The fake account that copies your brand with a "Christmas giveaway"

**How it works.** Someone creates an Instagram account almost identical to yours (one dot or one letter different in the name, your logo, your photos) and posts a giveaway or a promotion. They send private messages to your followers: "You won! To receive the prize, pay for shipping" or "send us your details". Your customers lose money and blame you.

**What to do.**

- **Warn people before it happens.** Post a highlighted story and a pinned post saying which is your only account and that you never ask for payments by private message.
- **Search for your name on Instagram** every so often, especially if you're running a real giveaway: that's when you get copied the most.
- **Report the account** from its profile, using the impersonation option, and ask your community to report it too.

For the highlighted story, you can use this text:

> This is our only official account. We don't ask for payments, card details or codes by private message. If another account with our name messages you, don't reply and report it.

## 2. The customer who "overpaid you"

**How it works.** They send you a receipt for an amount higher than the purchase, "by mistake", and ask you to refund the difference to another account. The receipt is fake, and you end up transferring them real money. A variation: the payment did come in, but with a stolen card. You refund the difference and weeks later the cardholder disputes the purchase and you lose everything.

**What to do.**

- Same as with any payment: **verify it in the app** of Mercado Pago or your bank, never in a screenshot.
- If you really were overpaid, **refund through the same method**: the refund or return option on the original payment. **Never transfer to another account** someone sends you by message.

## 3. The supplier who "changed their CBU"

**How it works.** At the end of the year a lot of invoices get paid. You receive an email that seems to come from a regular supplier, with their signature and logo: "We changed banks, from now on transfer to this CBU" (bank account number). Sometimes the email comes from the supplier's real account, which was hacked. The money ends up with a third party and the supplier keeps asking you to pay the invoice.

**What to do.**

- **No change of bank details is accepted by email or by message.** It's confirmed by calling the supplier at a phone number you already had saved, not the one in the email.
- Be wary of urgency and secrecy: "it's urgent", "don't call, I'm in a meeting".
- If you pay many suppliers, put that rule in writing for whoever handles the payments.

## 4. The "package on hold" notice

**How it works.** You get a text message, a WhatsApp or an email that seems to come from a postal service or a logistics company: "Your package is on hold, pay $1,500 to release it". The link leads to a page that asks for your card details. In December it works very well, because almost everyone is waiting for some package, the business included.

**What to do.**

- **Shipment tracking is checked on the official website**, typing the address and the tracking number yourself. Not from the link in a message.
- Let your customers know, with a message alongside the tracking number, that **you will never ask them for a payment to release a shipment**.

## 5. The one who "comes to pick up" with the payment "on its way"

**How it works.** A new customer places a large order, says they already paid or that the transfer "is delayed", and sends a courier or a cab to pick it up. With the shop full, someone on the team hands over the order. The payment never arrives.

**What to do.**

- A rule with no exceptions: **no order goes out without the payment credited to the account.**
- Make sure **the whole team** knows the rule, including the temporary December staff, who are exactly the ones scammers target most.

## The checklist before December 1

1. Highlighted story and pinned post with your only official account.
2. Two-step verification on Instagram, WhatsApp, Mercado Pago and the business email.
3. Written rule: payments are verified in the app, not in screenshots.
4. Written rule: suppliers' CBU changes are confirmed by phone.
5. A separate user for each temporary staff member, with the minimum access they need, and a calendar reminder to remove that access in January.
