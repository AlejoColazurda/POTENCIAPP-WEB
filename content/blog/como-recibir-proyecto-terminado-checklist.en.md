*"Buying software without getting burned" series · Chapter 7*

The message arrives: "It's ready, here's the link". It's the best moment of the project and also the riskiest. Whatever you don't check now, you'll discover with an angry customer on the other end. And whatever you discover after the final payment depends on the provider's goodwill (or on the warranty you signed).

This is the checklist we would use if we were on the other side. Set aside an hour or two, and do it **before the final payment**.

## 1. Test it the way your customer will use it

- **From your phone**, since that's how most people get in. And also from a computer.
- From **another browser and another network** (on mobile data, not just your home wifi).
- Go through **every page** in the menu. Tap every button.
- Check the text: prices, phone number, address, opening hours, names. Typos in the phone number are more common than you'd think.

## 2. Test the complete journeys, from start to finish

Looking isn't enough: you have to **do** what a customer would do.

- **In a store:** make a real purchase, with a cheap product and a real payment method. Check that the money arrives, that the customer gets the confirmation email and that you get the notification. Then cancel or refund it.
- **Contact forms:** send one and check which email it goes to, and that it arrives.
- **Sign-up and login:** create an account, log out, log in, request "forgot my password".
- **In an app:** install it from the store (not from a test link) on an Android phone and on an iPhone.
- **In a system:** run through a normal workday from start to finish: enter data, edit, search, pull a report.

## 3. Check what you can't see

- **Speed:** measure it on pagespeed.web.dev, on mobile. If the contract said something about performance, compare ([how to read the result](/en/blog/web-lenta-como-medirla)).
- **The padlock** (HTTPS) on every page, with no "not secure" warnings.
- **Google:** make sure the website isn't blocked from search engines. Leaving on the setting used during development is a common mistake. Ask them to confirm it.
- **Analytics** (Google Analytics or whichever was agreed) working, with you as the administrator.

## 4. Ask for what they have to hand over

This is what gets forgotten the most, and what hurts the most later:

- **All the accounts in your name:** domain, hosting, store, developer accounts for the apps. The full list is in [the 8 things that must be in your name](/en/blog/cuentas-a-tu-nombre-agencia-programador).
- **The code**, in a repository on your account, if it was custom development ([why it matters](/en/blog/quien-es-dueno-del-codigo)).
- **The passwords** for everything created for the project, in a secure place (not over a chat that gets deleted later), and change them as soon as you get them.
- **A how-to guide** for using the dashboard or the system: how to add a product, how to change a price, how to see an order. It can be a document or a video.
- Basic **technical documentation**: what technologies were used, where it's hosted, how a backup is made.

## 5. Put everything in writing

- Make a **list of what doesn't work or is missing**, with screenshots, and send it through a channel that leaves a record (email).
- Agree on **what gets fixed before the final payment** and what's left for later, with a date.
- Ask for the **warranty start date**: from when the period during which faults are fixed at no charge begins. Ideally it's in the contract ([the warranty clause](/en/blog/clausulas-contrato-programador)).
- If everything is fine, a simple **acceptance note**: "I received project X with the following pending items...". It protects both sides.

## What isn't a pending item

Watch out for the other extreme: a pending item is **something that was in scope and doesn't work**. A new idea that occurred to you when you saw the finished project ("what if it also had...?") is a **change**, and it's quoted separately. Keeping the two apart avoids most fights at the end of a project.

## The final payment, on delivery

If the contract is well put together, the final payment is made **once the delivery is approved**: not before testing it, and not in exchange for the accounts. The accounts and the code have to be in your name **beforehand**.

A good provider will welcome this checklist: it saves them problems too. If any of these points meets resistance, pay attention.
