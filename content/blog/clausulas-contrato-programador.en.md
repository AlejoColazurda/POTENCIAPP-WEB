*"Buying software without getting burned" series · Chapter 4*

In the previous chapters we saw what happens when nothing is in writing: the code isn't yours, the budget triples and nobody knows what was included. This chapter is the antidote: the nine clauses any development contract must have, whether it's for an online store, an app or an internal system.

The contract doesn't have to be long. It has to be **specific**. For each clause we show you what it looks like when it's weak and what it looks like when it works.

> This is not legal advice. If the project is big, have a lawyer read it. But you can check these nine things yourself in ten minutes.

## 1. Scope: what's included and what's not

- **Weak:** "Development of a complete online store."
- **Works:** a list of screens and features ("catalog with category filters, cart, payment with Mercado Pago, admin panel to edit products") and another list of what is **not** included ("product loading, shipping integration, English version").

The list of exclusions is the one that prevents the most arguments. If a provider doesn't want to write it, ask yourself why.

## 2. Milestones with something you can test

- **Weak:** "Estimated delivery: 6 to 8 weeks."
- **Works:** stages with dates, and each stage ends with something you can open and use yourself ("week 3: browsable catalog at a test address").

A progress report that says "we're doing fine" is not a milestone. A link you can open is.

## 3. Payments tied to milestones

- **Weak:** "100% upfront" or "50% at the start and 50% at the end," with an end that never arrives.
- **Works:** each payment is released when a milestone is approved. A reasonable down payment to get started, and the rest in installments that follow what is already working.

And **an invoice for every payment**. Without an invoice there's no proof of payment, and without proof of payment there's no claim.

## 4. Changes: how anything outside the scope gets quoted

- **Weak:** "Additional changes are charged by the hour."
- **Works:** "Anything not in the scope is quoted in writing, with a price and a deadline, **before** work starts. If you don't approve it, it doesn't get done." Plus a set number of revision rounds included in the design.

The word "hour" with no cap is a blank check.

## 5. Code ownership and delivery of the source code

- **Weak:** the contract says nothing (and as we saw in [chapter 1](/en/blog/quien-es-dueno-del-codigo), silence works against you).
- **Works:** "The rights over the development made for the client are assigned to the client once paid. The source code is delivered in a repository in the client's name."

## 6. Access and third-party costs in your name

- **Weak:** "The provider takes care of the hosting and the domain."
- **Works:** "Domain, hosting, Apple and Google accounts, database and payment gateways are created in the client's name. The provider accesses them as a guest user." Plus a list of the **recurring costs** (hosting, domain, licenses, apps) with their approximate amount and who pays them.

## 7. Warranty: what it covers and how fast they respond

- **Weak:** "Post-delivery support."
- **Works:** "For X months, any fault in what was delivered is fixed at no charge. Faults that take the system out of service are handled within Y hours." And it should say what it does **not** cover (new changes, problems caused by third parties).

A warranty with no response time is a promise, not a warranty.

## 8. Data and confidentiality

Your system is going to store your customers' data: names, phone numbers, addresses, purchases. In Argentina that falls under Ley 25.326 on Personal Data Protection.

- **Weak:** nothing.
- **Works:** "The provider uses the data only for the project, does not share it, and returns or deletes it when finished." Plus confidentiality over your business information.

## 9. Exit: what happens if either side walks away

The clause nobody wants to read, and the one that matters most when things go wrong.

- **Weak:** nothing, or "either party may terminate with 30 days' notice."
- **Works:** "If the contract ends early, the client pays for the work done up to the last approved milestone and receives the code, the access and the documentation for that work."

Back to the question from chapter 1: *if we stop working together tomorrow, what do I take with me?* This clause is the answer in writing.

## How to review a contract in ten minutes

Grab the contract they sent you and check each point:

1. Is there a list of what is **not** included?
2. Does each stage end with something I can test?
3. Do the payments follow the stages?
4. Are changes quoted in writing before they're made?
5. Does it say the code is mine and that it will be handed over to me?
6. Does it say the accounts are created in my name, and how much they cost per month?
7. Does the warranty have a term and a response time?
8. Does it say what they do with my customers' data?
9. Does it say what I take with me if we part ways?

If you're missing three or more, don't sign yet: ask them to add them. A serious provider will do it without any drama. If they get offended, that's information too.
