When someone searches "hardware store in Baradero" or "hair salon near me", the first thing that shows up isn't a website: it's the **map with three businesses**, with their stars, their hours and a button to call. Those three spots come from the **Google Business Profile** (what used to be called Google My Business).

It's free, you don't need a website and you can set it up in an afternoon. We did it ourselves this year, when we opened our office, so this guide comes from having done it, not from reading about it.

## Before you start: do you qualify for a profile?

Google gives profiles to businesses that **deal with their customers face to face**, at a location or by going to them. There are two cases:

- **You have a location** (a shop, a practice, a workshop): you show the address and you appear on the map.
- **You go to the customer's home** (plumber, electrician, pest control): you set a **service area** and hide the address. That way you don't publish your home address.

If you only sell online and don't serve anyone in person, Google won't give you a profile on Maps. In that case the path is different: your website and search engines.

## Step 1: create the profile

1. Go to **google.com/business** with the **business's** Google account (not an employee's or the agency's: [this has to be in your name](/en/blog/cuentas-a-tu-nombre-agencia-programador)).
2. Search for your business name. If a profile already exists (sometimes a customer or Google itself creates one), request ownership instead of creating a new one. Duplicate profiles step on each other.
3. Choose the **primary category**. It's what matters most for showing up in a search: if you're a "Hardware store", don't pick "Store". You can add secondary categories later.

**A common mistake:** putting keywords in the name ("López Hardware - Delivery - Best price in Baradero"). It goes against Google's rules and can get the profile suspended. The name has to be the same one that's on your sign.

## Step 2: verify

Google has to confirm that the business exists and is where you say it is. Depending on the case, it will offer you one or more methods: a short **video** showing the location, the sign and something that proves it's yours (the keys, the cash register, an invoice), a call or a text message, an email or a postcard with a code.

Tips for the video, which is the most common method today:

- Record it **without cuts**, starting from the street: the sign, the door, the street with some recognizable landmark.
- Go inside and show that you have access: open the cash register, the storeroom or the office.
- If the place was previously listed as another business (it happened to us: our office was listed as a perfume shop), tell Google that business **moved**, not that it closed, so you don't hurt the previous owners.

The review can take a few days. In the meantime, the profile doesn't show up publicly.

## Step 3: fill it out for real

A half-finished profile shows up less and convinces less. This is the list worth having complete:

- **Hours**, including holidays. Nothing brings worse reviews than showing up and finding the place closed.
- **Phone and WhatsApp**, and a link to your website if you have one.
- **Description**: what you do, for whom and where, in plain language. No promotions and no capital letters.
- **Real photos**: the storefront (so people recognize it when they arrive), the inside, the team and what you sell. No stock photos.
- **Products or services**, with prices if you can publish them.
- **Attributes**: whether it's wheelchair accessible, whether you take cards, etc.

## Step 4: reviews, without cheating

Stars are what people look at most before choosing. To get them:

- **Ask for them.** Most happy customers don't leave a review because it doesn't occur to them. From the profile you can copy a **direct link to leave a review**: send it over WhatsApp after a sale or a finished job, or print it as a QR code for the counter.
- **Reply to all of them**, good and bad. To a bad one, reply calmly and with facts. The person reading isn't the one who complained: it's your next customer.

And what you should **not** do, because Google prohibits it and can delete reviews or suspend the profile:

- Offering discounts, giveaways or gifts in exchange for reviews.
- Asking only the customers you know are happy for reviews and not the rest. This is called "review gating" and it's also prohibited.
- Buying reviews or asking family and employees to write them as if they were customers.

## Step 5: keep it alive

The profile isn't a "do it once and forget it" thing:

- Update your hours before every holiday and before your vacation.
- Upload new photos every so often and post updates (a new product, special hours).
- Answer the **questions** people leave: they show up publicly and anyone can answer them, even with wrong information.
- Check the **suggested edits**: any user can propose changing your hours or your phone number, and sometimes Google accepts them on its own.

## Security: don't let anyone take it from you

The profile is managed from a Google account. Turn on [two-step verification](/en/blog/verificacion-en-dos-pasos-comercio) and, if someone else manages it (an employee or an agency), add them as a **manager**, never as **primary owner**. If that person leaves, you remove their access and that's it.

## In short

A complete, verified profile with real reviews is the cheapest way there is to get found in your area. It doesn't depend on having a website, but the two work better together: the profile brings in the search and the website seals the deal.
