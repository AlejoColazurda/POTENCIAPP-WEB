Many businesses sell in two places at once: on **Mercado Libre** (the online marketplace), because that's where people are searching, and in **their own online store**, because there they don't pay Mercado Libre's fee and the customer stays with them. It makes complete sense. The problem shows up the day the same T-shirt sells on both channels and there's only one left in the warehouse.

This guide is so that doesn't happen, and so that selling on two channels doesn't cost you more than it brings in.

## The problem: two shop windows, one warehouse

Each channel has its own stock number. If you update them by hand, there's always a window of time when one of the two is out of date. With few sales, nothing happens. With many, or on a busy date, three things happen:

- **You sell something you don't have** and have to cancel.
- **You stop selling something you do have**, because "just in case" you loaded less stock on one channel.
- **You lose hours** every day updating numbers in two dashboards.

## Why a cancellation weighs more on Mercado Libre

In your store, cancelling a sale is a bad moment with a customer. On Mercado Libre, it also **affects your reputation**: sales cancelled for lack of stock count against you, and your reputation influences where your listings appear and the conditions the platform gives you. That's why, if you have to choose which channel to prioritize when stock is tight, it almost always pays to protect Mercado Libre.

## The golden rule: one place is in charge

Your stock needs **a single source of truth**. It can be:

1. **Your online store.** Many platforms have integrations or apps that sync stock with Mercado Libre: when something sells on one side, it's deducted on the other.
2. **A management system.** If you already use one for invoicing or for your physical shop, ideally it's in charge and both the store and Mercado Libre read from it.
3. **Mercado Libre**, if it's your main channel and the store is secondary.

What doesn't work is having "both in charge". Before installing an integration, ask yourself which of the three is the truth in your business.

## How to get it in order, step by step

### 1. A unique code per product (SKU)

Each product, and each variant (size, color), needs **its own code that's the same on every channel**. That's what lets an integration know that "Blue T-shirt M" in your store is the same one as on Mercado Libre. Without a SKU, any sync fails.

### 2. A safety margin

Even if syncing is automatic, there's always a gap of a few seconds or minutes. For products that sell fast or that you only have a few units of left, **list one or two units fewer** than you have. Selling one unit less is cheaper than cancelling.

### 3. Don't list everything everywhere

Your catalog doesn't have to be identical. Some businesses list on Mercado Libre only the products people search for most there, and keep bundles, packs or low-margin items for their own store. Fewer shared products means less risk of a clash.

### 4. Different prices per channel

Mercado Libre charges a fee per sale that depends on the category and the type of listing, plus whatever shipping costs you have to cover. Your store has its own costs ([here we explain how to add them up](/en/blog/cuanto-cuesta-tienda-online)). It's normal for **the same product to have different prices** on each channel. Work out what you actually keep on each one before setting them.

### 5. If you use Mercado Libre Full

With Full, part of your stock is **in Mercado Libre's warehouse**, not yours. Those units are no longer available for your store. Track that stock separately and don't add it to what you can sell on your own.

### 6. Check the sync every week

No integration is perfect: a listing that got paused, a new variant without a SKU, a connection error. Once a week, compare the stock of a handful of products in all three places (warehouse, store and Mercado Libre). Ten minutes of checking saves you an afternoon of cancellations.

## Before a busy date

Before a special date (the holidays, a discount event, Mother's Day):

- Count the real stock of your best sellers and correct the numbers.
- Raise the safety margin on your star products.
- Make a test purchase on each channel and check that it's deducted correctly on the other.

## When do you need something custom-built?

For most businesses, the platform's integration or a sync app is enough. Custom development starts to make sense when you sell on **more than two channels** (physical shop, online store, Mercado Libre, wholesale), when you have **prices by customer type** or when stock has to come from a system that no integration connects to.
