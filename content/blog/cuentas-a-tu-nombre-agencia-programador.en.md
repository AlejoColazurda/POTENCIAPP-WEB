---
title: Before you pay an agency or a developer: 8 things that must be in your name
description: The domain, the email, the ad accounts, the app in the stores. If they are in someone else's name, your business depends on that person answering the phone.
---

It's a story that keeps repeating: a business hires someone to build the website, the store or the ads. For a while everything works. One day that person stops replying, there's a dispute over a payment, or they simply close their business. And the owner discovers that **they can't get into anything**: the domain is in the developer's name, the ad account belongs to the agency and the app is published on someone else's account.

It doesn't take bad intentions for this to happen. Often it's convenience: "I'll register it myself, it's faster." The problem shows up years later.

These are the eight things that should be in your name (or your company's) **from day one**. Whoever works for you can have access to all of them, but you have to be the owner.

## 1. The domain

It's your website's address (`tunegocio.com.ar`) and also the address of your emails. Whoever controls the domain controls both.

- If it's a **.ar**, it's registered with NIC Argentina and the holder has to be **your CUIT** (tax ID) or your company's.
- If it's a **.com** or another one, it's bought from a registrar (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Hostinger and the like). The registrar account has to be yours, with your email.
- Watch the expiration date: if the renewal notice goes to someone else and they don't pay it, you lose the domain.

## 2. The business email

If you use Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, **the admin account has to be yours**. Email is the key to recovering almost every other account: whoever controls it can click "forgot my password" everywhere.

## 3. The hosting or the server

Where your website or your system lives (a hosting provider, Vercel, AWS, whatever it is) is a service paid every month. Ideally the account is in your name, with your own payment method, and whoever does your maintenance gets in as a **collaborator**. If the account belongs to the provider, the day they stop paying for it, your site goes down.

## 4. The code

If something was custom-built for you, the code is part of what you paid for. Two things to ask for:

- That the contract or the quote clearly states that **the code is yours once it's paid for**.
- That it's stored in a repository (for example, GitHub) **on your account or your organization's**, and that the provider works there as a guest.

Without the code, any future change has to go through the same person, or start from scratch.

## 5. The app on Google Play and the App Store

Apps are published from a **developer account**: Google Play Console and the Apple Developer Program. The app "lives" in that account, along with its reviews and downloads.

- If it's published on the agency's account, the store lists it as the agency's.
- For a company, Apple asks for a D-U-N-S number, which is free but takes a few days. It's best to apply for it before the app is ready.
- Moving an app from one account to another is possible, but it's a process that depends on the current owner cooperating.

## 6. Instagram, Facebook and the Meta ad account

At Meta, everything hangs off the **business portfolio** (what used to be called Business Manager): the page, the Instagram account, the ad account and the pixel that tracks sales.

- The portfolio has to be yours, with you as admin.
- The agency comes in as a **partner** with the permissions it needs, not as the owner.
- The ad account, with your card. That way the campaign history, which is worth a lot, stays with you.

## 7. Google: Ads, Analytics and Search Console

Same logic. The Google Ads account, the Google Analytics property and Search Console have to be created with your email, with you as admin. Agencies usually manage everything from their own manager account, and that's fine, as long as **the linked account is yours** and you can unlink it whenever you want.

## 8. Where the money comes in

The Mercado Pago account (or whatever payment gateway you use) and the online store account, if you use Tiendanube, Empretienda or Shopify, have to be yours. The credentials that connect the store to Mercado Pago are generated **from your account** and handed to the provider, not the other way around. Every so often, go into the settings and confirm the money is being deposited where it should be.

## How to ask for it without it being awkward

Asking for this isn't distrust: it's what any well-organized company does, and a good provider gets it right away. If you like, send this message as is:

> Hi, I'm getting the business accounts in order. Could you send me a list of everything that's registered for us (domain, hosting, ad accounts, repository, developer accounts) and whose name each thing is in? The idea is for everything to be in the company's name and for you to keep accessing it as collaborators, just like now.

If the answer is evasive or they tell you "it can't be done", that's a sign to pay attention to.

## If something is already in someone else's name

Don't panic. Almost everything can be transferred: .ar domains have an ownership transfer process at NIC Argentina, Meta lets you move assets between portfolios and apps can be moved between developer accounts. What you do need is the other person's cooperation, so **it's best to do it now, while the relationship is good**, and not on the day it ends.
