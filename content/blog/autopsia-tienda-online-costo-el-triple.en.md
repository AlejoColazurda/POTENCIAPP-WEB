*"Buying software without getting burned" series · Chapter 2*

> **About this case.** It's a composite built from situations we hear about often from shops that come to us after a bad experience. The names are made up and the amounts are expressed as a percentage of the initial budget, so they stay useful regardless of inflation. We have seen every one of these wounds for real, each on its own.

Marina has a health food shop with a storefront and wants to sell online. Someone passes her the contact of a developer "who builds online stores." She writes to him on WhatsApp, he asks what she sells and how many products she has, and a little later he answers with a number: **the complete store**, ready in three weeks.

Marina accepts. Let's call that number **100**.

Six months later, the store has cost her **310** and hasn't sold anything yet. This is the autopsy.

## Wound 1: "The complete store" (week 1)

**What happened.** The quote was a single message: "Complete store, with cart and Mercado Pago." It didn't say how many pages, who would upload the products, what would happen with shipping or how many rounds of changes were included.

**Why it hurts.** "Complete" isn't a scope: it's an expectation. Each of them had a different idea of "complete," and both were reasonable.

**How it could have been avoided.** A written scope with a list of what it **does** include and, just as important, what it **doesn't**. If an item isn't on the list, it isn't included.

## Wound 2: "Product upload wasn't included" (week 3)

**What happened.** The store was ready, but empty. Marina had 420 products with weight variants. "I build the store for you; you upload the products, or I'll upload them for you separately." Uploading them separately: **+35**.

**How it could have been avoided.** Asking beforehand: *who uploads the catalog, how many products does it include and with what photos?* It's the cheapest question in the whole project.

## Wound 3: "Shipping is a separate integration" (week 5)

**What happened.** Marina wanted to calculate shipping by postal code. That required connecting with a shipping company: **+40**. On top of that, the hosting the developer had chosen started charging in dollars and billing her without warning: **+15** in the first six months.

**How it could have been avoided.** The quote should list the **recurring costs** (hosting, domain, apps, paid plugins) with their approximate amounts and in whose name they're paid. A quote that only shows the cost of building the store, and not of keeping it running, is incomplete.

## Wound 4: the changes that never end (weeks 6 to 10)

**What happened.** Marina asked for design changes. Some were done for free, others were charged "by the hour," with nobody knowing how many hours that was. Total: **+60**, paid in small pieces through scattered transfers, with no invoice.

**How it could have been avoided.** A set number of included revision rounds and a clear rule: anything outside the scope **is quoted in writing before it's touched**. And an invoice for every payment: without an invoice there's no proof of anything.

## Wound 5: the silence (week 12)

**What happened.** The developer got a salaried job and began answering less and less. One day, a plugin stopped working and the cart broke. Marina discovered that the domain, the hosting and the connected Mercado Pago account were all in his name.

Recovering the domain, migrating the store to an account of her own and fixing the cart with another provider: **+60**.

**How it could have been avoided.** With all the accounts in her name from day one (we explain it in [chapter 1](/en/blog/quien-es-dueno-del-codigo)) and with a written warranty: a period during which faults in what was delivered are fixed at no charge.

## The final tally

| Item | Cost (budget = 100) |
| --- | --- |
| Initial budget | 100 |
| Product upload | +35 |
| Shipping integration | +40 |
| Unforeseen hosting (6 months) | +15 |
| Changes "by the hour" | +60 |
| Rescue and migration | +60 |
| **Total** | **310** |

And what doesn't fit in the table: six months without selling online, and an owner who now distrusts every provider.

## What the autopsy shows

None of the wounds was bad luck. They all share the same origin: **an agreement that wasn't in writing**. The developer wasn't necessarily a scammer: he was probably someone with good intentions and no process. For Marina, the result was the same.

Before accepting a quote, ask yourself these five questions:

1. Do I have a written list of what's included and what isn't?
2. Do I know who uploads the content and how much is included?
3. Do I know how much I'll pay each month to keep it running, and in whose name?
4. Do I know how many changes are included and how anything outside that is quoted?
5. Is there a written warranty, and are all the accounts in my name?
