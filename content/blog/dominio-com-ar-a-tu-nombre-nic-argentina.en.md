The domain (`yourbusiness.com.ar`) is probably your business's most important digital asset, and the one that gets the least attention. It's the address of your website, but also of **all your emails**. Whoever controls the domain controls both.

And yet it's very common for it to have been registered by "the guy who made the website", in his name, with his email. While everything works, it doesn't matter. The day that person doesn't answer, it matters a lot.

## Who manages .ar domains

Domains ending in **.ar** (.com.ar, .ar, .net.ar, etc.) are managed by **NIC Argentina** (the registry for .ar domains), at **nic.ar**. It's an official body: you don't need to go through an intermediary to register one, you can do it yourself directly.

Registration is paid **once a year** and has to be renewed. Check the current fee on the NIC Argentina website.

## Step 1: check whose name yours is in

Before registering anything, check what's going on with the one you already have:

1. Go to **nic.ar** and look up your domain in the domain search.
2. The public lookup shows you **the holder** and the **expiration date**.

If the holder is you (or your company), perfect: skip to step 4. If someone else is listed, keep reading.

## Step 2: register a new domain in your name

If the domain you want is available:

1. Go to **nic.ar** and log in with your digital identity. The site will tell you the current method (for example, your ARCA clave fiscal, the password for Argentina's tax agency, or Mi Argentina).
2. Search for the name and, if it's available, register it.
3. Pay the fee.

The holder has to be **you or your company**, with your CUIT (tax ID). Don't use the developer's or a relative's CUIT or user "because it was easier".

## Step 3: if it's in someone else's name, ask for the transfer

NIC Argentina has a **change of holder** procedure: the current holder starts the transfer and you accept it from your account. It needs the other person to cooperate, so it's best to do it **now, while the relationship is good**.

A simple message is enough:

> Hi, I'm getting the business's accounts in order. Could you transfer the holder of the domain yourbusiness.com.ar on NIC Argentina to me? You'll keep managing the website just like now, only the holder changes.

If the person doesn't answer or refuses, and you can prove the name is yours (it's your brand, your company name), NIC Argentina has mechanisms for disputes. It's a longer road; if you've reached that point, also consult a lawyer.

## Step 4: connect it to your hosting without handing it over

For your website and emails to work, the domain has to "point" to your hosting provider. That's done through **delegation**: in your NIC Argentina account, you enter the **DNS servers** your provider gives you (they're addresses like `ns1.provider.com`).

**The important part:** for your provider to get the website working, **the domain doesn't need to be in their name**. It's enough for you to enter their DNS servers. You remain the holder, and if you switch providers tomorrow, you change the DNS and that's it.

## Step 5: don't let it expire

This is the most common way to lose a domain: **it expires and nobody renews it**, because the notice went to an email nobody checks or to a person who no longer works with you. After a while, the domain becomes available and **anyone can register it**, even someone who then asks you for money to give it back.

To keep that from happening:

- Make sure the **email on your NIC Argentina account is one you read every day**.
- Put the **expiration date** in your calendar, with a reminder a month before.
- Renew in good time. Don't wait until the last day.

## What if it's a .com?

Domains like .com, .net and similar aren't registered with NIC Argentina but with an international **registrar** (there are many). The logic is the same: the registrar account has to be **yours**, with your email, your payment method and [two-step verification](/en/blog/verificacion-en-dos-pasos-comercio) turned on. And watch out for auto-renewal: if the card on file expires, so does the domain.

## In short

- The domain, **in your name and with your CUIT**.
- The account, **with your email**.
- Your provider works with the **DNS**, not with the ownership.
- The expiration date, **in your calendar**.
