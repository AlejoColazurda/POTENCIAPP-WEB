title: "Fake emails and texts from ARCA and banks: how to spot them before you click"
description: A debt you don't have, a password that "expires today", an account that's "blocked". How the fake messages businesses receive work, the signs to detect them and what to do if you already clicked.
---

If you have a business, you have a CUIT (tax ID), a bank account and an email address that appears everywhere. For a scammer, that's enough to put together a believable message: an email "from ARCA" (Argentina's tax agency, formerly AFIP) saying you have a debt or a formal notice, or a text "from the bank" warning that your account has been blocked.

The goal is always the same: to get you to open a link and **type your clave fiscal (your tax-agency password), your online banking password or your card details** on a page that looks like the real one and isn't. Or to get you to download a file that installs something on your computer.

## What these messages usually look like

### The ones pretending to be ARCA (formerly AFIP)

- "You have a **debt** / a **formal notice** / a **garnishment**. Download the details."
- "Your **clave fiscal expires** today. Update it from this link."
- "You have a **pending notification**", with a button to "view it".
- An **attachment** (a .zip, an .html or a "receipt") to "see the details".

### The ones pretending to be banks or digital wallets

- "We detected a **suspicious transaction**. If it wasn't you, log in here."
- "Your account has been **blocked**. Validate your details to unblock it."
- A text with a short link and pressure: "today", "within 24 hours", "final notice".
- A follow-up call from someone who says they're "from the security department" and asks you for a code or to make a transaction to "protect" your money.

## The signs to detect them

1. **The pressure.** "Today", "final notice", "it will be blocked". The pressure is there so you don't think.
2. **The sender.** Look at the full address, not just the name that shows up. ARCA's official addresses end in **arca.gob.ar** (or afip.gob.ar). An "ARCA Notifications" sending from a Gmail address or from a strange domain is fake. Even so, the sender can be faked: it being right isn't enough to trust it.
3. **The link.** On a computer, hover the mouse over it without clicking and see where it goes. On a phone, press and hold. If the domain isn't exactly the agency's or the bank's, don't go in.
4. **They ask for information they already have.** ARCA and your bank already know your CUIT, your name and your account number. If they ask you to "confirm" them, be suspicious.
5. **They ask for passwords or codes.** No government agency or bank will ever ask you by email, text, WhatsApp or phone for your clave fiscal, your online banking password, your token or a verification code. **Never.**
6. **Unexpected attachments.** A .zip, an .exe or a file that asks you to "enable content" is not a receipt.

## The rule that saves you almost every time

**Don't go in from the message.** If they tell you that you have a debt or a notification, open your browser, **type the address yourself** (arca.gob.ar or your bank's) or use the official app, and check there.

- ARCA's real notifications are in your **Domicilio Fiscal Electrónico** (your electronic tax mailbox), inside the site, behind your clave fiscal. If there's nothing there, the email was fake.
- If the message is from the bank, call the number **on your card** or on the official website, not the one in the message.

## If you already clicked or typed something

Don't sit with the doubt: act in this order.

1. **If you typed your clave fiscal:** go to ARCA through the official address and **change it** right away. Check your enabled services to make sure there's nothing you didn't authorize yourself.
2. **If you typed bank or card details:** call the bank on the official number, tell them what happened and ask them to **block** whatever is needed. Change your online banking password.
3. **If you downloaded and opened a file:** disconnect that computer from the internet and have it checked before using it again for banking or for ARCA.
4. **Change your email password** too, if it's the same one you used on the fake site, and turn on [two-step verification](/en/blog/verificacion-en-dos-pasos-comercio).
5. **Warn your team and your accountant**: if it reached you, it will probably reach them.

## What to set up today

- **Two-step verification** on your email, your bank and all your important accounts.
- **A different password** for your clave fiscal, your online banking and your email. If one leaks, the others stay safe.
- **Transaction alerts** turned on in your bank's app, so you find out the moment something happens.
- A rule for your team: **nobody pays, transfers or enters passwords because of a message** without confirming it first through another channel.
