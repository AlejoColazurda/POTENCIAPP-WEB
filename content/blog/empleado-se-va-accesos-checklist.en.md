When someone stops working at your business, they hand back the keys to the shop. But the digital keys are almost never handed back: their online store user is still active, the business WhatsApp is still open on their computer and the Instagram password is still the one they knew.

Most of the time nothing happens. But when it does, it's serious: a former employee who can still see your sales, who deletes something out of spite or, more commonly, an account of theirs that gets stolen months later and becomes the way into yours.

**This isn't about distrusting anyone.** It's what any well-run company does, even when the person leaves on the best of terms. Do it **the same day** the working relationship ends.

## Before you start: build the list once

The hardest part is knowing **what they had access to**. If you don't know, build a simple table now with three columns: service, who logs in and how (their own user or a shared password). That table will serve you for this departure and for every one that follows.

## The checklist

### Email and documents

- If they use a business account (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365), **suspend it**, don't delete it yet: that way you keep their emails and files.
- Transfer their documents and shared files to another account.
- Set up an auto-reply or forwarding so customer emails don't get lost.
- If they had access to a shared account (for example, sales@), **change the password**.

### The business WhatsApp

- In WhatsApp Business, check **Linked devices** and log out their computer or phone.
- If the business number was on a phone they're taking with them, get it back before they leave.
- Make sure [two-step verification](/en/blog/verificacion-en-dos-pasos-comercio) has a PIN and an email that are **yours**.

### The online store

- In Tiendanube, Empretienda, Shopify or whichever you use: **delete their user** from the users or staff section of the dashboard.
- If they logged in with your user (something best avoided), change the password.
- Check that they haven't changed sensitive details: the notification email, the account where payments are deposited, the contact details.

### Payments and money

- In **Mercado Pago**, remove the permissions or the user they had.
- At the **bank**, if they were an operator or signatory, request their formal removal.
- Check the **card terminals and card readers**, and the registers or point-of-sale systems: remove their user.
- If they had a company card, cancel it.

### Social media and advertising

- In Meta (Instagram and Facebook), go to the **business portfolio** and remove their access to the page, the Instagram account and the ad account.
- If social media was run with a shared password, **change it** and turn on two-step verification with a phone of yours.
- Do the same in Google Ads, Google Analytics and your [Google Maps profile](/en/blog/google-business-profile-aparecer-en-maps).

### Systems and tools

- The management, stock or invoicing system.
- Work tools: Canva, shared Drive, task managers, the CRM.
- Shared **password managers**: remove them from the vault and change the passwords they could see.
- If they had access to ARCA (Argentina's tax agency) as a delegated user, revoke the delegation from your clave fiscal (your tax-agency password).

### The physical stuff

- Keys to the shop and the warehouse.
- **Alarm code**: change it, or delete their personal code if the system allows it.
- Business laptops, phones or tablets: have them returned, and check that none of their personal accounts are still signed in.

## The detail almost everyone forgets

Check **which phone receives the verification codes** for each account. If, when two-step verification was set up, the employee's phone was used, now they're the one receiving the codes to log in to your account, or to recover it. Change it to a number of yours on every service.

The same goes for **recovery emails**: if any business account has that person's personal email as its recovery email, change it.

## To make the next departure easier

- **One user per person, always.** Almost every service (store, Meta, Google, Mercado Pago) lets you add people with their own user and permissions. That way, removing someone means deleting a user, not changing ten passwords.
- **Accounts in the business's name**, never in the employee's name ([the same as with a provider](/en/blog/cuentas-a-tu-nombre-agencia-programador)).
- **Keep the access table up to date**: every time someone starts using a new service, add it.
